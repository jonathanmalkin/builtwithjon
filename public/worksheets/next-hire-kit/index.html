The Next-Hire Training Kit

Every new hire learns the job from your head. Again. The material to change that already exists. This kit turns it into one training module someone can run without you.

One job, one module. Not the whole role. The second module is the new hire's job, and that is the point.

Part 1The material inventory

Pick one job you keep training from scratch. List everything that already teaches it. Recordings of you explaining it. Documents, even rough ones. Past work that was accepted. The email or chat threads where you answered the same question. Most owners find more than they expected in under an hour.

Part 2The one-module template

Fill this in for a single task inside the job. Point at sources from Part 1 instead of rewriting them. A module is a map to the material plus one exercise and one check, not a textbook.

Module title · one task, done by one person

Done well looks like · describe the accepted output and attach a real example

Sources to point at · three to five items from the inventory, with where

The walk-through · steps in order, each decision with your rule of thumb

One exercise · a real, recent, small example the new hire does

One check · what you or a senior look at to call it right, and the two most common mistakes

Part 3The handover rule

  1. The new hire runs module one using the sources, does the exercise, and you run the check. Every question they asked you along the way goes into the walk-through.
  2. The new hire builds module two for the next task, using this template and the inventory. You review it; you do not write it.
  3. If they cannot build module two, look at the gaps list. The missing piece is almost always a decision rule that lives in your head. Record it once, ten minutes, and add it to the inventory.

A module written this way is also exactly what a well-set-up AI assistant needs to answer the next new hire's questions from your material, with a source they can check. The material first, then the assistant.
