The Repeat-Questions Log

Your team asks you the same questions every day. Most of the answers already exist. This sheet finds the ones worth fixing first.

Fill in the log during the week, not from memory on Friday. Memory keeps the dramatic questions and drops the two-minute ones, and the two-minute ones are the problem.

Part 1The one-week log

Every time a question lands on you, write one line. Their words, not your summary. Print a second copy if you run out of rows. Leave the last column blank until Friday.

DayWho askedThe question, in their wordsWhat they neededMinPile

Friday count: ____ questions, ____ minutes. Most owners land between twenty and forty questions for the week and are surprised how few are new.

Part 2The three-pile sort

On Friday, mark every line A, B, or C. Be strict about A. If you would give the same answer every time, it is A even when the person asking thinks it is complicated.

Pile A · Approved answer exists

A fact, a location, a price, a policy, a decision you already made. You would answer it the same way tomorrow. This is the only pile you fix this month.

Pile B · Needs your judgment

The answer depends on the case and you weigh something to decide. Do not try to fix these yet. Write down what you weigh; that note is the beginning of training someone else.

Pile C · Nobody actually knows

There is no settled answer. Decide once, write the decision down, and it moves to pile A.

Count: A ____ B ____ C ____. Pile A is usually the biggest. That is good news. It means most of the interruptions are retrieval problems, not judgment problems.

Part 3The first ten

From pile A, pick the ten questions with the most minutes times the most repeats. For each one, fill in the four lines below (use the back of the page for the rest). Then follow the three rules.

The question, in their words

The approved answer, or a link to it

The one place it lives now

Who keeps it current

  1. One place, and it already exists. The shared folder your team opens every day, the pinned message in the channel they read, the notes field in the system they live in. Do not create a new place for this.
  2. Point, do not answer. Next time one of the ten lands on you, send the place instead of the answer. It feels slower for a week. It is the whole fix.
  3. Recount in two weeks. Run the log again for three days. Count how many of the ten still reach you. Any that do have a wrong place or a stale answer, not a stubborn team.

Once the ten answers live in one place, an AI assistant that reads that place can answer the next hundred with a source your team can check. The place comes first.
